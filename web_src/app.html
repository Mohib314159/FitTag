How it works, what's real, and what's next

How it works

  1. Find the sheets. OpenCV finds four printed markers whose positions are known to the millimetre — they give both the scale and where the garment is.
  2. Flatten. A homography from those 16 corners removes the camera tilt: every pixel becomes 0.5 mm.
  3. Cut out the garment. The backdrop colour is measured at the edges; GrabCut refines the outline.
  4. Measure like a tailor. Row by row along the outline: waistband, the row where the legs split, armpits, hems. No model outputs a number.

What's real, what isn't

  • Every number here is this repo's Python running on these photos.
  • The photos are rendered, not shot: cloth albedo plus a height map in millimetres, lit by one light, then a virtual phone camera with tilt, vignette, noise and JPEG.
  • Rendering them properly exposed six bugs the old flat test shapes never hit — a crash on any non-white background, the crotch found at a stitching gap, armpits at the neckline, jeans called a top, one-sheet photos a third too small.
  • It refuses to measure when the garment blends into the floor instead of guessing.

Limits

  • Never yet run on a real photo of a real garment against a tape measure.
  • Shoulder width reuses chest width, so it's left out of fit checks.
  • Low contrast between garment and floor shortens the inseam; the app warns.
  • Card mode (no printing) gets scale right but not framing — below.

Build log

  1. Jul 2026
    Built at the Fleek × a16z hackathon. Printable sheets, flattening, measuring, fit verdicts, API.
  2. Sep 2026
    Photoreal test photos, six bugs fixed, 8× faster (25 s → 3 s per photo), refuses low-contrast shots.
  3. Sep 2026
    No printing needed. A plain sheet of A4 is 210 × 297 mm everywhere, and as a ruler it lands within 1% of truth. Framing was the part that needed a person — shadows and folds look like denim to a threshold — so the user drags a box round the garment and everything downstream works: median error 0.8 cm, worst 3.5 cm, against 0.5 cm for the printed mat.
  4. Sep 2026
    Card mode, experimental. A bank card is 85.60 × 53.98 mm worldwide, so scale works — rectified through a detected card, the card measures back at 85.2 mm. Framing doesn't: the sheets also fix orientation and crop, so segmentation grabs shadow. In the repo, off by default.
  5. next
    A real photo against a tape measure, published whatever it shows.
  6. next
    Tap-the-corners on a phone. Mark a card or a blank A4, drag a box round the garment: no printing, and the geometry is already exact.
